# ToyVentor vision

This is the internal, long-term side of the vision. For the hackathon story,
see [submission-vision.md](submission-vision.md). For today's behavior, see
[spec.md](spec.md).

## Grow into robotics through play

Kids lose interest when toys arrive finished and cannot change. ToyVentor keeps
the toy alive: the child can rebuild it, take it into a shared digital world,
and turn it into something new.

## Three tiers, one system

Snap gives younger children snap-fit parts; Motion gives older children and
makers mechanical parts; Pro is the real-robot tier for the oldest builders. All
three share one TU lattice, one verification gate, and one app bridge. Committed
catalog targets are 106 Snap + 79 Motion parts.


## The play experience

One screen holds scanning, building, forging, playing, creating, and sharing.
Toybox keeps toys, Forge improves them, the game plays equipped builds, Create
makes new content, and parent gates protect time, content, purchases, and
printable files. The child’s real build is the save file: equipped parts
change stats, paint changes identity, and missions remember what was tested.


## Access and business

The app can be free while physical toys, kits, replacement parts, and printed
or ordered upgrades carry the business. Subscriptions can unlock new toys,
exclusive content, marketplace access, and creator powers.

## Principles

- One screen first; focused sheets only where depth is needed.
- Real builds drive digital play; digital success motivates physical making.
- Sharing, purchases, printable files, and play limits stay parent-controlled.
- Play alone first; multiplayer and creator sharing follow only with approval.

## Near-term proof

Today the Toybox works. Next come profiles, AR, ordering, and creator tools.
Content can be added, adjusted, or retired without changing scanning, building,
playing, creating, or sharing. The 90-second demo must already prove the loop:
a scanned toy becomes playable, printable, and shippable.
