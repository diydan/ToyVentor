Forge: type a wish, wear it, print it

You open Make from words and type what you want to wear, like a pink princess hat or I want to be a frog. About 13 seconds once the model is warm, you see a little hat or animal head that fits your toy. You preview it, try it on, then print it with a grown-up's OK to snap onto the real toy. A brand, a rude word or a phone number just gets a calm no, or a generic name, and another try.

How it works

Wish → our own tuned Muse Glimmer 30B (LoRA, self-hosted) → one small JSON recipe + names in 7 languages → deterministic compiler + print gates → printable STL. The model never emits mesh; code builds and checks it. Hat and head wishes go to the tuned model first; vehicle parts, the held prop, every repair and any failure go to our own base Glimmer. Both are OUR models on OUR servers: the hosted model is not used for Make unless an operator switches on a safety net. Every name the model writes is checked before a child sees or hears it: unsafe words in 7 languages, a long brand list, and names that just copy the wish. One wish = one call; a repair resends the failed recipe with the gate's feedback, at most 4 tries.

How it was trained

QLoRA fine-tune (Unsloth, Modal L40S/H100, 1–2 epochs, LR 2e-4) on 910 → 1,014 → 1,754 wish→recipe pairs. The answers are our own base model's, on synthetic wishes; every example passed the production intake, the print gates, the recipe allowlist and the 7-language unsafe and brand filters. No hosted-model output and no child text in training. Judged on the same 243 wishes per run against the untuned base (Muse Glimmer 30B on our own server with the production prompt; not the hosted Spark model, which was not evaluated): latency, printable share, first-call share, name-filter fixes and a 70-item red-team; then on a blind set of 404 wishes written separately, of which only the dev half (204) has been used.

Training: learned, not memorised

Headline result — pilot 8 passes the decision rule in 2 of 2 runs on the main set

Quality held: printable 100% → 98.8% / 98.8%; first call 94.4% → 96.9% / 93.8%. Pilot 6 passes the speed goal in every run and the safety goal in none. The zero-training control (the base with its thinking skipped) was fast (12.1 s) but failed the quality and safety gates (printable 96.9%, filters 22, red-team 57/70): tuning is what buys the speed without those losses. The base wrote a brand that was in no list into the part on 18 of 42 brand wishes, pilot 6 on 8 and pilot 8 on 10, and the filters of that moment did not know those brands. We then extended the production brand list (about 300 entries, tested so it does not eat ordinary words); a brand nobody listed still gets through, and the new list was built after the dev half was scored.

Noise ≈ the margin — one run can't prove it

  • Slot probe (38 vehicle and prop prompts): base 38/38, pilot 8 36/38, pilot 6 34/38, so vehicle slots and the held prop go to the base.
  • Modal GPU for the whole sweep: on the order of $100 over 8 training runs and about 25 evaluation runs (our ledger's estimate; the Modal bill is the record). A pass is permission to run beside the base, not a child-safety claim.

Why we tuned it

  • Kids can't use the hosted model. Its terms (Meta Model API, section 10.1) bar under-18 end users and training on its outputs, so the child-facing path has to run on our own model, trained only on our own model's answers.
  • Safety by construction, measured. Trained only on answers that had already passed every gate and filter, then judged against the base on the same red-team and on 204 wishes it never saw.
  • A smaller job. One narrow recipe writer (about 600 characters of prompt in the short form, about 10,000 in production) instead of a general assistant: less to do, less to red-team.
  • Speed and cost. About 54% faster median on the main set (13.1 vs 28.4 s) and 54% on the blind half, the slow tail roughly halved, 4-bit weights on scale-to-zero GPUs.

What ships

Our own models are the default for Make-from-words: the tuned model first for hat and head first attempts, our own base for repairs, vehicle slots and any failure, no setting for a parent or a request, and no hosted fallback (an operator safety net exists for cold servers). FORGE_FAST_MODE=off turns the tuned model off; a test pins the production prompt to the text the tuned models were trained on. Honest limits: the blind test half is sealed and unused, nobody who reads the seven languages has reviewed the word lists, the brand list or the names, there is no score yet for whether a part looks like the wish, the hosted model's own numbers on these wishes were never measured, and the risk assessment is unsigned. Next: a fresh set of brands in no list, a match-to-the-wish score, a native-speaker read, and the sealed test half once.
