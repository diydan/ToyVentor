# Design log

A dated record of the design decisions behind this system, kept as evidence of independent design. Each entry says what was decided and why. The generator code, the specs' changelogs and the selftests hold the detail; keep the whole folder under version control (git) so every change is dated, and never rewrite history.

The geometry is our own throughout: our lattice, our stud and hole dimensions, our fit numbers. Existing construction systems were looked at only as background research; no files, dimensions or code from them are used, and a selftest fails the build if a third-party construction-system name appears in any file.

## On or before 2026-10-28 (earlier sessions)

- **Two lines on one lattice.** Snap (young children, studs) and Pro (ages 8 and up, pins and bores), both on a 12.5 mm unit (TU). Small studs at 1 TU, Large at 2 TU, the Pro hole grid at 1 TU. Chosen as our own unit, unrelated to any existing system's pitch.
- **Single source of truth.** Every number, check level and safety constant lives in `interfaces.json`; code and documents are generated from it.
- **Check system.** Geometry, printability, interface, safety and metadata checks, with severities as data. Physical checks (printed fit, breakage) are always pending until a print is tested.
- **Shared Forge Mount.** One mount definition for both lines, attaching to the scanned toy and exposing a stud or bore face.
- **Safety screen.** A conservative small-parts screen against the 31.75 mm cylinder; the Large size is the only one meant for under-3s.

## 2026-10-28

- **Add-ons re-proportioned** to the lattice (body scale 1.5625); wing roots buried in the foot to remove an overhang.
- **Status is derived, not declared.** A part is verified only when no blocking check fails, the small-parts result is not marginal and a Large part clears the cylinder.
- **Fit is measured.** Parts are sliced at mid-engagement and each stud or pin gap is measured from the built geometry. A cavity 0.5 mm too loose had passed the old overlap-only check; it is now blocked. Fit-critical cylinders moved to 96 segments because 32-segment facets were as large as the 0.05 mm design gap. Calibration target: printer surface offset within about -0.07 to +0.08 mm.
- **End game set:** the top 30 Snap and 30 Pro parts, tracked in `catalog.json`; one catalog row per part.
- **Round block replaced by a dome.** A round outline cannot hold a stud grid on the lattice (it needs 28.4 mm against a 24.8 mm footprint).
- **Small slope at 30 degrees.** At 45 degrees it landed in the marginal small-parts band.
- **Pro rules enforced:** bores on the lattice and really present, pins and bores built to the interface, gears whose pitch diameters are whole TU, and pin-and-bore mating in the validator.
- **Pro core:** beams, pins, shafts and bushings. Pins and shafts print lying on a flat for strength; parts may print in a different orientation than they assemble.
- **Snap characters and capsules** built from size-parametric builders; the small Forge Mount uses a 5x5 grid so a 2x2 part grips four studs.
- **Gears, rack, ring gear, corner beams, Pro mount and the Snap-to-Pro adapter.** The worm pair was dropped after testing: a module 2.5 worm needs supports at 20 and 30 degrees and has no tooth left at 45. Replaced by an internal ring gear and a Z25 spur gear.
- **Round Forge Mounts** sized from the scan, refused if a 2x2 part would grip fewer than 3 studs.
- **Integrated bases.** Add-ons and capsules no longer stand on a plate: stud holes are cut into their own underside or a small pad, direction-sensitive parts are locked against turning, and every one is checked on a full plate.
- **Legal review.** Licensing drafted (`LICENSING.md`), compliance checklist written (`COMPLIANCE.md`). Name screen: the current product name is already used by others in toys (an AI sketch-to-printable-toy tool, and an action-figure maker's brand), so a replacement name needs clearing before launch.
- **Hugging Forge Mount, two parts.** Tested a one-piece hugging mount on 12 stand-in surfaces: 11 needed supports, and a one-piece seat touched only 13 to 60 percent of the surface. Chose a shoe that prints hugging-face-up (no supports, full contact) joined to the usual mount face by friction pins. Large faces join permanently, because removable pins are small parts and the Large line is for under-3s.
- **Clip-on shoes for limbs and tails.** The shoe's C wall wraps a limb up to 240 degrees and snaps on; the wrap is chosen so the estimated strain stays under 2% and each arm opens at least 0.25 mm (five times the calibrated print error). The first version's clip was missing because a cutting shape was wound the wrong way; a render caught it, and a geometric test (the limb must sit free but not drop out) now guards it.
- **BlockML and MachineBlocks evaluated, not adopted.** A spike and a pilot looked at both. The MachineBlocks geometry files carry non-commercial share-alike headers despite the package's Apache-2.0 declaration, and BlockML did not enforce rules across connected parts. No code, files or outputs were carried over (see `WHY_NOT_BLOCKML_MACHINEBLOCKS.md`).
- **Specs rewritten clean (v2.3)** with the current state; build history moved to this log. New Forge Mount spec; `specs/` is assembled by `make_specs.py`.
- **W5 done; catalog complete (60 of 60 verified).** Wheels, a hinge leaf and a cross connector. Connectors may now point along any axis and parts may tip in quarter turns, so builds can have horizontal axles and hinge pins. Horizontal free bores print as teardrops and fixed bores flats-up with 50 degree shoulders. The gate caught three slips along the way: a teardrop drawn with a coarse circle (gap 0.193 against 0.200 mm), the fit matrix measuring sideways bores as if vertical, and a shaft seated with its flats crosswise to a fixed bore (0.75 mm interference). Seating now lines up flats.
- **Lattice decided (Dan, 2026-10-28).** Snap's 8/16 mm pitch became 12.5/25 mm on 2026-10-27 as a proposal that was implemented and built on but never formally confirmed; Dan confirmed it today. Reasons: it is the only way Snap and Pro share one lattice without permanent adapters; 8/16 mm matched the spacing of the market-leading brick systems without matching their studs, which invites a fit expectation it cannot meet; Large parts clear the small-parts cylinder comfortably at 25 mm (16 mm cleared it by 14%); bigger studs tolerate home-printer error better. Cost: Large parts are about 1.6x the size of a toddler brick at the old spacing and use about 3.5x the material. Condition: at the calibration print, a printed Large 2x2 must feel right in a toddler's hand and on a real toy; if it is too big, shrink Large on the same lattice rather than return to 16 mm.
- **Renamed to ToyVentor (toyventor.com), Dan, 2026-10-28.** The earlier name was already used by others in toys. A web search found no toy product using ToyVentor; the nearest names are a toy-collector magazine called Toy-Ventures and a new toy venture called ToyVerse. A web search is not a trademark search: run one with the USPTO before launch. Entries above this one use the earlier name as it stood at the time.
- **Pre-print validator (2026-10-28).** `preprint_check.py` checks any STL before printing: ten geometry checks (including layer-by-layer islands, bridges measured between supports, features narrower than the nozzle, and wall thickness by ray casting), then a real PrusaSlicer slice for filament, time and stability warnings, with automatic brim escalation. One JSON report per part for the app. On the catalog: 43 pass, 9 need a brim, 8 have warnings, none fail. It found what the generator's gate cannot see: stud holes nearly breaking through on Cloud Wings and Pebble Armor Shell (both sizes), a thin floor on the Bounce Capsule, a knife-edge ridge on the Small slope, and bed adhesion needing a brim on pins, shafts and three capsules. Tuned against false alarms (dome tops, sharp edges, tube-supported roofs).
- **Fixes from the pre-print validator (2026-10-28).** Stud holes on the Cloud Wings (both sizes) opened into the V between the wings; every full integrated stud hole now gets at least 1.2 mm of roof (a boss clipped to the footprint where the shape is too thin), proved by a new block check I7_hole_roof on all 15 parts with integrated holes. Slopes got a 1 mm front lip and a 1.6 mm flat top (no knife-edge ridge); the Ridge Fin's apex rounding, commented out by mistake, is restored. The validator's wall-thickness rule now ignores readings where the two sides meet at a nearby edge, which removed a false alarm on the Bounce Capsule. Catalog: 47 pass, 11 need a brim, 2 have slicer warnings for review, none fail; all 60 parts verified.

- **Bridge versus ledge (2026-10-28).** Closed the gap logged with the Critters set: a flat underside now counts as a bridge only if every point has support on both sides along a straight line within 20 mm, or lies within 1 mm of support. One implementation (toyventor_ledge.py) runs in the generator (P4_no_ledges, block) and the pre-print check (V10_no_ledges, block). Proven on test shapes (a 6 mm shelf and a 24 mm bridge caught, a 16 mm bridge passed, the old tail caught). On the catalog it found small real lips where a body's square edge overhung a rounded base: both slopes, the Hood Scoop and both tails now flare up from the base's real outline (shared flare_clip). An older, unwired copy of the same check found in the working tree was removed, so the rule lives in one place. All 70 parts pass; pre-print: 56 pass, 14 need a brim, none warn or fail.

- **Proportions tuned to measured references (2026-10-29).** Dan uploaded two models and asked to use them as a base. Their licence and provenance were unconfirmed (one looks derived from a protected character), so they were used for measurement only: nine ratios per model (proportions.py), stored as numbers in reference_proportions.json; no geometry, texture or shape is kept. The comparison showed our heads too deep, our bodies about twice too wide, and the widest point at 62% up the head against about 37%: a balloon, not a dumpling. - **Pro humanoid: 15 snap-in ball joints (Dan, 2026-10-29).** The articulated version of the Snap core, our own geometry and numbers (pro_humanoid.py). Same layout as Snap, every joint a ball: neck, chest, waist, 2 shoulders, 2 elbows, 2 wrists, 2 hips, 2 knees, 2 ankles. 16 printed parts. Printing rule that makes it supportless: every part prints upright, its SOCKET opens on the flat bottom (the bed face) and its BALL stands on top, so each limb is a chain where the ball below plugs UP into the socket above. The ball is a droplet: a 38 degree cone tangent to the sphere under it. The socket is a cavity (lower sphere, a 38 degree cone ceiling tangent to the sphere so it clears the ball, a 30 degree lead-in chamfer), with a 0.6 mm moat round its cup and a cross of 0.8 mm slits so four petals flex to let the ball in. Starting values (to calibrate): radial clearance 0.25 mm, opening 0.80 of the ball. Balls 10 mm (neck, chest, waist), 8 (hips, shoulders), 7.5 (elbows, knees), 6 (wrists, ankles); about 30 degrees of swing each (an estimate). Three bugs found by checking, not by looking: the droplet formula put its cone inside the sphere (steep underside); unioning the full sphere with the cone left the dome as the ceiling (steep, and it overlapped the ball by 70 mm3); a 45 degree chamfer was an overhang. All 10 unique parts then print clean and the assembled figure has zero overlap. Real validator: all 12 parts pass, 11 with a 3 mm brim (the sockets cut away bed contact). The figure is 135 mm tall, about 59 g and 6.5 hours. Calibration kit gained Test E: a plate of six sockets (clearance 0.15 / 0.25 / 0.35, opening 0.72 / 0.80 / 0.88) and a plate of six matching balls. Not yet tested on a printer: whether the petals flex and snap, or the swing range. Weak: shoulders (66 mm yoke) and the pelvis read as flat rings; 15 joints at this size make fat limbs; Pro heads are the Snap heads with a socket, so they are not interchangeable with Snap (a stud-to-ball adapter would fix that).
- **Pro base: adapt the CC figure instead of rebuilding it (Dan, 2026-10-29).** Dan asked why our Pro humanoid could not match the original ball-jointed figure. Measured: 14 of 22 ratios were within 15% (limbs, balls); the gaps were the head (1.73 x too wide, a style choice), the torso and shoulders (our rule that every socket opens on the bed forced a yoke and a pelvis that read as rings), and fat arms (snap-in sockets need walls, a moat and slits; the original's captured balls do not). With the original usable, adapting it beats reinventing an untested print-in-place joint. Built (make_reference_rig.py): 36 bodies simplified from 982,000 to about 57,000 triangles (one very dense body clustered), grouped into 14 bones with Mixamo-style names (Hips, Head, arms, legs) that pivot at its own ball joints, three clips (Idle, Wave, Walk) within 30 degrees, GLB validates with 0 errors. Caveats: bone names match Mixamo, but Mixamo clips assume a T-pose rest and spine, neck and shoulder bones this rig lacks, so retargeting is needed; the joints' real range is unmeasured; the derived mesh is not in the repo until the variant and author are confirmed. Next: print the original as it is to prove the Pro base on your printer.
- **Pro customiser: proportions, constraints and heads (Dan, 2026-10-29).** Dan asked for longer/shorter arms, legs and torso, bigger/smaller hands, feet, torso and head, with functional constraints, and three more heads (human, robot, cat). Key decision: JOINT HARDWARE NEVER DEFORMS. A smooth stretch or scale field says where a point would move, but any point within r_out of a joint centre (ball radius + 2.5 mm, blended over 5 mm) takes that joint's rigid displacement instead, so every ball, the cup round it, and the neck connector move as rigid pieces and no clearance can change. Only the shaft between joints stretches. The same formulas run in the page (pro_deform.js, preview) and in Python (customize_pro.py), which edits the full-resolution 3MF by moving vertices only (same 982,418 triangles and 36 parts, so it opens like the original). Verified: JS vs Python agree to 1e-14 mm; at the extremes every joint partner moves with its ball to 0.003 mm; on the real file, 8 joint clearances (hip, knee, elbow, wrist, ankle, shoulder, neck) are unchanged to 3 decimals while the figure grows from 188 to 214 mm; PrusaSlicer opens the result as a manifold with 36 parts. Ranges: arms and legs 0.80-1.30, torso length 0.85-1.25, hands and feet 0.85-1.40, torso width 0.90-1.25, head 0.80-1.35. Constraints: ranges are refused by the command-line tool before anything is written; a live stability check (centre of mass inside the feet) never failed in 400 random combinations at and between the limits (smallest margin 11.7 mm, so the ranges are conservative). Three new heads (Human, Robot, Cat) from our own generator, sized for this figure (preset 'ref', 42 mm wide), are PREVIEW ONLY: the original's neck is a captured connector with 0.2 mm clearance to the head and torso, and a head that prints around it is not designed yet. Do that after the original has been printed and its neck joint confirmed. A bug found by the headless test, not by reading: three.js removes the colon from node names, so mixamorig:Head becomes mixamorigHead; the earlier Pro viewer's pose sliders would have thrown errors, and the new page replaces it. Tests live in tests_js/ (integration and interface, run in Node). Not measured: the real range of each joint and how a stretched limb prints; the limit values are estimates.
- **Pro heads redesigned to match the body (Dan, 2026-10-29).** Dan said the new heads did not match the body's aesthetic. Diagnosis from a full-resolution study of the original head: it is a hard-surface astronaut helmet (smooth dome, a large curved visor with a bezel, a hood-like outer shell scooped round each ear, circular pivot pods with concentric rings) in ONE cream material. Our first heads were soft chibi eggs with painted-on faces in skin and hair colours: wrong in both form and material. Rebuilt (pro_heads.py) as one helmet family: dome shell with a flat base, a flare under 40 degrees, vertical sides and a squared dome; a 0.8 mm hood with a chamfered scoop round each pod; a visor (robot, cat) or faceplate (human) with a bezel; stepped 40 degree pods; one cream material with small tonal steps (coral only for the pods and the antenna, the joint colour). Human: hair hood, brows, eyes, stepped nose, mouth. Robot: visor with eyes and a grille, antenna ball. Cat: slanted visor eyes, whisker bars, bold triangular ears. Printing: all three pass the real validator as 'Prints as is' (no brim, no supports, no warnings), about 17 g and 1.1 to 1.2 hours each. Lessons, all found by checking and not by looking: (1) my renderer drew parts as separate layers, hiding thin details, so it showed missing eyes that existed: sort every face together; (2) skins that merely touch the shell do not always fuse: let each sink 0.25 mm in; (3) curved cut edges tilt through the steep zone: use chamfered polygons, and check their orientation after a rotation; (4) the validator reads a float32 STL, and in-memory geometry hid slivers that only appeared after rounding (a wedge left between a window edge and a scoop corner; brows straddling the faceplate edge): float32_islands() now replays the validator's layer logic in the repo's own checks. Heads are still PREVIEW ONLY on the figure: the neck connector between them and the printed body is not designed.
- **Pro faces made cute (Dan, 2026-10-29).** Dan: heads better, but the facial features were not cute enough. The first faces had small eyes, flat brows, a nose and a serious mouth. Rebuilt to the brand's face language (BRAND_STYLE.md: big head, eyes low and wide, expression left to the decal or game skin, never moulded): big round eyes set low and wide, each with a white glint; the spark colour (#FF6B4A) as blush on the cheeks; a tiny neutral mouth; no brows and no nose on the human (the cat keeps a small spark nose and a tiny omega mouth as its species marker, and two whisker bars).
